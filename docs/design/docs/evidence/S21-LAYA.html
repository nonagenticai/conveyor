State: built. Measured at 7d56abb2 on 2026-11-24, before the PR. The merge is the lane's last CI run; nothing in this receipt is claimed for main yet.

What changes at this level

Before: ladder.evaluate took answer, confidence, latency_ms and probabilities from its request body and wrote rung = 'laya', so any governance:write caller could record a calibrated-looking L1 decision it had invented; options were inline request text; an outage could not become gate_unavailable; and nothing could stop the case being asked.

After: the ladder builds the case from the effective policy and an authored, versioned option set; the engine performs the request as the external action ladder.precheck; ladder.evaluate records a decision only from the engine's recorded exchange (the claim's request hash, a 2xx status, the invocation's measured duration); the answer must be an authored key; an unavailable answer follows the policy's fail action; and a policy whose fire_when does not hold is not asked at all.

Notes and fixture amendments (recorded, not hidden)

  • decision.input_hash and meta.case_sha256 are the case's sha256. The engine's idempotency witness is a different hash (idempotency::request_hash, an FNV-1a claim hash); the P1b cut had used it as input_hash, which the column's sha256 check refuses. The case now carries both: Case::hash (the claim) and Case::sha256 (the decision).
  • The executor now uses the shared builder. It previously constructed its own judge::request and derived the claim from that copy; decide() now calls case::build, so the authored menu and fire_when reach an in-process gate too.
  • The parity samples were rewritten, not relaxed. ladder.evaluate's sample now presents a claim no engine call backs and both transports must refuse it (422); the verified happy path is ladder_laya.rs's, since a parity sample cannot call a real laya. decision_option_sets.set is sampled live (the set it authors is read by .get in the same round).
  • Two ladder test helpers changed shape. decision_ladder's evaluate now drives the verified path with a stub whose verdict it controls, and uses a distinct draft text per verdict: two evaluations of an identical case share the claim and the engine replays the first exchange — that is the verification working, not a fixture shortcut.
  • A deterministic parity sample cannot use prepare (that branch of the harness runs none); the read is chained to the set the authoring sample just wrote.

Residuals (kept, not fixed here)

  • Automatic apply of an L1-accepted draft (ADR 0011:77) — the decision now exists honestly; what happens to an accepted draft is its own unit.
  • The live model/laya proof remains blocked on the estate's caller licence (S21-RUN.md); every proof here is stub-backed and the tests say so.
  • The SOP judge (laya.judge, process/step.rs) keeps its own question and threshold.
  • Mid-loop budget enforcement is the #526 lane's; release pinning (v1-p42-1), prompt-budget authoring (v1-p77-2) and the NULL-hash guard (v1-p77-1) are untouched.
  • A tenant-authored fire_when / question-type UI; both are authored through the actions.
