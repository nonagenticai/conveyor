P6.2 — one review, end to end

Three short-lived Keycloak users in the conveyor realm (reviewer-a, reviewer-b, lead-reviewer) and one client (evidence-review-cli: public, direct-grant only, audience mapper conveyor, realm role review-lead on the lead) were created through the master admin for this proof. Tokens were minted by direct grant and carried in X-Actor-Token; none is recorded here, and the client and users are removed after the run. Access tokens live five minutes, which the first attempt outlived — see "Aborted attempts".

Every step below was issued through kubectl port-forward svc/scientific-processor 18808:8808, as the lead unless marked otherwise.

The flow ledger, the sealed manifest and the export's own counts agree, which is the invariant the whole family exists for: a published inclusion set that a later read contradicts would be the failure, and it is not present.

Aborted attempts, recorded

Two earlier invocations of the same script failed and wrote nothing that the final run relies on:

  1. 401 the presented actor token could not be verified at the first call. The tokens had outlived Keycloak's five-minute access lifespan while the earlier steps were prepared. Re-minted immediately before the run.
  2. A run against the same review whose assignment step failed because the script read /tmp/sub-a while the bootstrap writes /tmp/sub-reviewer-a. Requests reached the service and were refused (422 assignment, 404 decision without an assignment); the review itself (01a1f019-2c4d-…) remains in the tenant with an empty corpus and no decisions, and the proof above uses a distinct review (01a1f019-b40a-…). Recorded rather than deleted: reviews is append-only in spirit and archiving it is the disposal path.

What this does and does not prove

  • It proves the merged binary, the migrated schema, the non-owner RLS role, the licence tenant gate and the verified-user rung work together on a real review with two blinded reviewers, one conflict, one adjudication and one sealed export.
  • It does not prove response equality against the retired service: no recorded responses exist (capture deviation 3). P5's parity suite proves operation coverage and the drop table; this run is the end-to-end replay.
  • The proof identities are removed after this document is written; the Deployment keeps its actor configuration, and an unconfigured realm refuses every actor token by design.
