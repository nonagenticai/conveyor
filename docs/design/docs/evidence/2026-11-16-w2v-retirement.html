The dead w2v estate retired — 2026-11-17

Part of #213. Its single blocked-by (#205, the research cutover) closed on 2026-11-17 at 14:30Z; the deployment half merged the same evening as the deployment repository's PR 5323 (22d4b10), Flux reconciled, and this document records what changed.

What was wrong. The 17 Deployments in namespace w2v had reported 0/0 with no pod for weeks, and the 16 Services answered only those dead backends. studio.nonagentic.ai therefore returned 503 — envoy's canned upstream connect error — instead of a deliberate status. A host that answers 503 into nothing looks like an outage that nobody is watching; a 404 is a statement.

What was removed, and what deliberately was not

Removed Count Why
services/w2v/deployments.yaml 17 Deployments 0/0, no pod, 137–192 days old
services/w2v/services.yaml 16 Services every one answered only the above
services/w2v/poddisruptionbudget.yaml 1 PDB set over Deployments that no longer exist
the replicas: transformer + ~800-line patches: block — every patch targeted a Deployment removed here
services-istio/w2v/virtualservice.yaml → VS w2v 1 VS it routed the two hosts below at the dead backends
services-istio/w2v/destinationrule.yaml 1 DR its only subject was w2v-frontend

Kept, and stated in the kustomization: the namespace, pv-worker3.yaml, storage.yaml (the data), the default-deny networkpolicy.yaml, the ConfigMaps and the Secrets, and the w2v-legacy-redirect VirtualService (which carries no backend — it is why w2v.nonagentic.ai still answers at all). This unit deletes no data; every manifest is one revert away.

The gate consequences this unit produced

Removing 34 objects moved the deployment repository's non-vacuity floor: build-all.sh measured 1925 → 1891 objects, below its 1900 floor. The floor moved to 1880 with its reason recorded beside it, and the alert-coverage baseline — a ratchet, not a permanent exemption — lost its 17 now-orphaned w2v/* entries rather than being silenced. build-all.sh at the merged head: 13 roots, 1891 objects, 0 violations.

A clusters/** merge fires no workflow by design (#3930); the PR's validate job ran on the pull request and passed, which is the run that matters.

The third consequence: a dark DNS name

Removing the w2v VirtualService left the namespace's in-cluster DNS name in the master's dnsmasq pointing at the gateway VIP with nothing behind it. The next pull request to touch scripts/host-configs/** surfaced it:

ERROR [dnsmasq] <the name> points at the gateway VIP (<the address>) but has NO VirtualService,
no coredns-static-hosts entry, and is not in GATEWAY_VIP_NON_VS — it is a DARK name

That check is right, and it is the reason a removal is not finished when the manifests go: a name that still claims to exist but answers 404 is worse than a name that is gone. The record was removed in the deployment repository's PR 5325, and check-host-config-consistency.py then reported 0 error(s), 0 warning(s) across 5 hosts.
