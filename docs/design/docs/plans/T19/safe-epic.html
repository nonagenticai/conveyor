T19 — SAFe epic

Epic

Hypothesis. If a cross-department decision is convened as a typed meeting whose agenda items resolve approvals and whose participants record positions, then the programme can show why a decision was made, who dissented and what was promised — so decision latency falls, rework from unrecorded objections falls, and a forum that decides nothing is visible and retired.

Outcome. The §12 T19 gate: a three-department majority with recorded dissent; an inquorate deferral; no lowering of dual control; the owner decides live and over MCP; minutes behind the chair's gate; every action a card; a decision-free forum flagged.

Non-goals. A chat product; an AV or calendar integration; an LLM participant; a second decision store; a rebuild of T9's board or T10's cadence.

Demo evidence

The system demo exhibits: a scheduled meeting; an agenda materialised from an approval and a register item; an inquorate deferral; a three-department majority with a verbatim dissent; a commitment that becomes a dependency; an action that becomes a card; a minute published behind the chair's approval; the owner's two decisions (web room and MCP) as one row each; and the participant-hours figure. No demo, no credit.

Inspect & adapt

The retro reads decision latency, agenda conversion, quorum-miss, commitment slippage, action closure, person-hours per decision and decisions per meeting. A systemic finding becomes a kata candidate (T5); a policy finding becomes a meeting_type change through the governance route, not a code edit. A forum that decided nothing over the cycle is the first candidate for retirement.
