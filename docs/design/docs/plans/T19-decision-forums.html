T19 — Decision forums (meetings)

Programme project: T19 · issue #606 · close-out #650

Gate (SPEC_V2 §12 T19): a three-department board meeting decides its agenda items by recorded majority with a recorded dissent; an item missing a required department is deferred inquorate and cannot be decided; a meeting cannot lower a linked approval's dual control or waive its evidence; the owner decides one item in the web meeting room and another asynchronously from the agent front end over MCP, both recorded via='meeting'; minutes publish behind the chair's gate and every action becomes a card; a forum with no decision over a cycle is flagged.

1. Objective

The conveyor records the decision and the deliberation. High-level work — programme management, architecture, analysis, project management — happens in meetings between positions from different departments; today only the resulting approval row survives. T19 makes the meeting a first-class card whose agenda items resolve existing approvals, whose participants are job bindings or humans holding typed positions, and whose outcomes are derived by arithmetic over those positions and the linked approval's stricter policy. The record is rows, not a transcript: the meeting adds the cross-department positions, conditions, commitments and dissent, and never creates a second decision store.

OUT — a chat system, a transcript store or an AV product; a calendar dependency (ICS is a read of attendance); an LLM that selects items, decides, infers a position or marks attendance; a second decision store; a rebuild of T9's board, T10's cadence or the approval primitive; the T18 UI lane; ArchiMate-style rendering of the minutes.

The route /meetings and /meetings/:id are declared in the spec; T18's shell lists the destination, T19 owns the screen and its meetings.spec.ts.

4. The model

4.1 The meeting, the card and the participant extension

A meeting is a card (its chair owns it, WIP limits and DoD apply) plus a meeting row. Its DoD is every agenda item closed with an outcome and the minutes published. The convener is a governance_body, a job_binding or an app_user; one live meeting exists per cadence slot (uq_meeting_live).

The approval primitive's decision row is human-only today: approval_decision.approver_id uuid NOT NULL REFERENCES app_user(id). §6.8 requires one decision row per qualifying seat, and seats are both humans and job bindings. T19 therefore extends the primitive rather than inventing a second decision record:

  • approver_id becomes nullable; add job_binding_id uuid REFERENCES job_binding(id);
  • add participant_kind text NOT NULL DEFAULT 'human' CHECK (participant_kind IN ('human','job'));
  • a CHECK requires exactly one of approver_id, job_binding_id;
  • the existing unique (approval_id, approver_id) stays; add a partial unique (approval_id, job_binding_id) WHERE job_binding_id IS NOT NULL;
  • existing rows backfill participant_kind='human'; decided_by_kind='agent' for job seats.

The alteration is additive and tenant-scoped like the existing table. It is the one core-table change in this slice; the alternative — writing a meeting decision into the L1 decision table — would create the second decision store §6.8 forbids.

4.2 Policy and convening

meeting_type is the forum policy: purpose, agenda_sources[], required participation (departments and positions with minimum counts), quorum, decision rule (unanimous | majority | chair), evidence minimum, decision window, timebox and allowed outcomes. It is tenant-scoped and versioned (P11): a meeting pins the type version it was opened under, so a policy change never rewrites an in-flight forum. Who may convene is an authority row, not code.

4.3 The agenda is an L0 query

The item set is L0 over agenda_sources[] — open approvals for the body, register items of named kinds, dependency slips, cadence slots — plus explicit meetings.agenda_add. A deciding item names exactly one pre-opened approval with its evidence and policy; there is no implicit approval. An item without the type's evidence minimum is refused 422 item_evidence_missing. The LLM drafts wording and the pre-read pack; the chair adopts the agenda. It never selects.

4.4 Attendance and positions

meeting_attendance holds the participant (job_binding or app_user), seat (chair | member | presenter | observer | delegate), department and invitation/response state; delegation is allowed only where the type permits it. meeting_position is one live row per participant per item: stance (approve | approve_with_conditions | reject | abstain | request_evidence), rationale, conditions, optional commitment, surface and time. The position is immutable once the item closes. An observer's position is recorded and never counts.

approval_decision.decision admits only approve | reject; a position of approve_with_conditions writes an approve decision row and the conditions stay in the position, the approval's meta and the minutes. The derived item outcome can still be "approved with conditions" because it is computed from the positions, not copied from the decision column.

4.5 The outcome arithmetic

close_item derives the item outcome in this order:

  1. the attendance quorum over the type's required participation; a missing required department defers the item inquorate;
  2. the meeting's rule over the cast positions: a majority counts cast positions, abstentions count toward quorum only, a tie defers, chair carries once quorum exists and every required department has stated a position;
  3. the linked approval's policy: required decisions, distinct approvers, evidence minimum, P4 conflict exclusion and dual control;
  4. the stricter result wins. The meeting never lowers dual control, never waives evidence and never bypasses P4. An observer never counts. A participant whose own work the item judges is excluded by the same conflict rule.

On a deciding close, one approval_decision row per qualifying seat is written with via = {"surface":"meeting","channel":"web|telegram|mcp","meeting_id":...} (the JSON via column already exists). meeting_position is never a decision row.

4.6 Outcomes, commitments, actions and minutes

The closed set is decision | commitment | action | information | deferred. A decision is the linked approval's recorded result, derived from the decision rows. A commitment names the committing position, beneficiary, what, by when and its condition, and becomes a project_dependency edge or a card; no owner or date = refused. An action becomes a card (§6.6). An information item is typed and counted. No outcome = cannot close. Minutes are a governed_asset(kind='minutes') drafted from the rows and published behind the chair's approval; the rows remain the record, and a minute line that does not match a row is a defect.

4.7 The human seat, live and asynchronous

Any human, including the owner, holds an ordinary app_user attendance row in any seat. Every deciding item is also an approval with a decision window, answerable from the web room, the web UI, Telegram or the agent front end over MCP. via records the surface; the first decision across surfaces counts and later ones are refused by the existing (approval_id, approver_id) unique rule. No calendar system is depended on: meeting_attendance is the record, and an ICS feed is a read of it. Absence is recorded as absence, never invented as an approval. A conversational room over MCP is the agent front end's surface; the conveyor supplies the items and records the positions, so the record does not depend on the room.

4.8 Metering and retirement

Participant-hours derive from attendance × duration and enter D52's roll-up as a third source beside inference_call and tool_call, at tenant/department/programme/project/job grain. The instruments are decision latency, agenda conversion, quorum-miss rate, commitment slippage, action closure, person-hours per decision and decisions per meeting. A meeting_type whose instances produce no decision over a cadence cycle is flagged and retired, exactly as a never-refused gate is flagged (P10).

6. Data model

Five new tables, all tenant-scoped, ENABLE+FORCE RLS, with created_at/updated_at/version:

Table Columns and constraints
meeting_type key, name, purpose, agenda_sources jsonb, required_participation jsonb, quorum int, decision_rule text CHECK (unanimous|majority|chair), evidence_minimum int, decision_window interval, timebox interval, allowed_outcomes text[], status, version; unique (tenant_id, key, version); a version is immutable once a meeting pins it
meeting card_id, convener_kind CHECK (governance_body|job_binding|app_user), convener_id, meeting_type_id, meeting_type_version, opens_at, decision_window_end, cadence_slot; partial unique (tenant_id, meeting_type_id, cadence_slot) WHERE status='live'
meeting_agenda_item meeting_id, sequence, resolved_kind CHECK (approval|register_item|asset|cadence), resolved_id, participating_departments uuid[], evidence_refs jsonb, outcome text CHECK (decision|commitment|action|information|deferred), defer_reason; unique (meeting_id, sequence)
meeting_attendance meeting_id, participant_kind CHECK (job_binding|app_user), participant_id, seat CHECK (chair|member|presenter|observer|delegate), department_id, invitation_state, response_state, delegated_from; unique (meeting_id, participant_kind, participant_id)
meeting_position meeting_id, agenda_item_id, participant_kind, participant_id, stance CHECK (approve|approve_with_conditions|reject|abstain|request_evidence), rationale, conditions jsonb, commitment jsonb, surface CHECK (web|telegram|mcp), recorded_at; partial unique (agenda_item_id, participant_kind, participant_id) WHERE superseded_at IS NULL; immutable once the item closes

Two alterations, both additive:

  1. approval_decision: nullable approver_id; add job_binding_id uuid REFERENCES job_binding(id) and participant_kind text NOT NULL DEFAULT 'human' CHECK (participant_kind IN ('human','job')); CHECK exactly one participant; partial unique (approval_id, job_binding_id) WHERE job_binding_id IS NOT NULL; existing rows keep participant_kind='human'.
  2. governed_asset_kind_check: widen with minutes.

approval_decision.via is already jsonb; a meeting decision writes {"surface":"meeting","channel":"web|telegram|mcp","meeting_id":"<uuid>"}. No other V1 table is altered. card.job_binding_id exists (T0); project_dependency (T1), governance_body (T9) and programme_cadence (T10) are reused.

7. Actions and events

Every action is an ActionSpec with one declaration → HTTP + MCP + OpenAPI + generated client. The family is meetings.<noun>_<verb> because the registry requires exactly one dot:

Action Effect Notes
meetings.schedule write creates the meeting card + meeting in scheduled; policy-version pinned
meetings.open write scheduled → live; emits meeting.opened in one transaction
meetings.invite write creates/updates attendance; authority-checked
meetings.respond write invitation response; never invents a position
meetings.agenda_add write explicit item; evidence minimum; exactly one approval
meetings.position write one live position per participant/item; draft-only LLM cannot call it
meetings.defer write deferred outcome with a reason (inquorate included)
meetings.close_item write writes per-seat approval_decision rows; derives the outcome; emits meeting_item.closed
meetings.close write closes the meeting; refuses while an item has no outcome
meetings.minutes_publish write publishes the governed asset behind the chair's approval; emits meeting_minutes.published
meetings.list / meetings.get read invitations, room, history, instruments

Events are one subject and one verb; the writing action and its event commit in one transaction. architecture.board_open|get|decide remain T9's macros over these actions and add no decision path.
