P0 — Programme definition

Programme project: P0 · issues #120, #121, #122

Track: programme · Capabilities: architecture, programme management, project management, business analysis · Gate: the definition set is published and adopted; no V2 delivery project starts before it.

1. Objective

Design the programme that delivers the full conveyor, following MSP's Defining a Programme and Realizing the Benefits: the mandate, programme brief, blueprint, business case, benefit profiles and benefits map, projects dossier, programme plan and tranches, governance, stakeholder profiles, benefits management strategy and realization plan, reuse map and project briefs. P0 is a project of the programme it defines, so every artefact in this set is an intermediate output stored on P0 in the console and published on the design site.

The programme does not start T1–T17 until this set is accepted. The gate is the programme's, not a document's: a delivery project's brief is the contract it is held to, and a benefit's profile is the claim its transition is measured against.

2. Starting state (measured at the P0 baseline)

  • V1 is far implemented: 25 measured-built slices, 2 partial (S14, S21); the Rust workspace, the action registry, the board/card/run/approval/document aggregates and the public design pack.
  • T0's organisation spine merged (#65, migration 0040, organisation.* actions, release hash and binding pin); its measured coverage row is corrected by this unit's seed change.
  • The programme seed has 47 projects and no programme-definition project. T1–T17 are proposed and blocked by their SPEC_V2 §12 edges.
  • docs/SPEC_V2.md is the design record; docs/SPEC.md is the V1 build record. Neither is a programme plan; P0 produces the plan that adopts them.

3. The definition set (the project's outputs)

  1. Mandate — strategic fit, current position, drivers, deliverables and boundaries, delivery and assurance, success and constraints. Approved by the sponsoring group.
  2. Programme brief — outline vision, benefit categories, cost/timescale/effort, risks and stakeholder support, delivery options including doing nothing, candidate projects and timing, and an assessment of current operations and performance.
  3. Blueprint — the target architecture and operating model: aggregates, services, the action registry, the data model, the deployment estate. The to-be the projects deliver.
  4. Business case — why now, the benefit map, the options considered including doing nothing, the investment appraisal and cash flow, cost/risk posture, how the investment delivers the capabilities the benefits depend on, and the public/opt-in licensing frame.
  5. Benefit profiles and benefits map — one profile per benefit (§6), and the map from project output → capability → operational outcome → benefit/disbenefit → strategic objective. The public/opt-in licensing frame is a condition of the benefits, not a footnote.
  6. Projects dossier — every project with its outputs, resource requirements, timescales, interdependencies, initial requirements, high-level budget, contribution to the blueprint and the risks affecting delivery.
  7. Programme plan and tranches — the definitive project list, sequencing, the tranche plan (§7), entry/exit criteria and the review dates. It adopts and updates the existing seed; it does not invent a parallel list.
  8. Governance & organisation — roles (sponsoring group, SRO, programme board, programme office, programme manager, programme architect, BCM, PMO lead, quality manager), the decision ladder, assurance, the unit protocol, and the strategy/plan set: benefits management, information, risk and issue, monitoring and control, communications, quality, and resources. The baseline trio — management, governance and boundary — is recorded with the definition.
  9. Stakeholder profiles — concerns, sensitivities, importance, influence, interest, exposure, support level and trend, the engagement target, and the benefits and disbenefits distributed to each group.
  10. Benefits management strategy and realization plan — measurement methods and sources, the review cycle, who assesses realization and when, transition and handover dates, embedding activities, and who continues measurement after programme closure.
  11. Reuse map — what exists and may be reused: the conveyor V1 tree (tables, actions, modules, processes), the estate's absorbed services and modules, the seeded catalogues, the upstream crates. For each: reuse candidate, boundary, licence/contract constraint.
  12. Project briefs — one per delivery project: outcome, boundaries, dependencies, acceptance, capabilities, plan link, reuse sources to analyse, and the two inception units below.
  13. Architecture / analysis pack — current-state analysis, options, risks, dependency map, and the architecture artefacts the blueprint rests on, produced to the architecture artefact standard (SPEC_V2 §8.1).
  14. Architecture capability baseline (the Preliminary phase) — the six things an architecture project needs before it starts: the architecture principles catalog (statement, rationale, implications per principle); the tailored ADM profile (which phases run, which artefacts are mandatory per phase and plateau, which contract closes each phase); the artefact standard adopted with its repository and type catalogue; the Architecture Board charter, membership and decision limits; the governance, contract and waiver baseline; and the maturity (nine dimensions, 0–5) and skills (seven categories) baseline with its development milestones. It is the programme's default adm_profile; a delivery project's brief says which plateau its own architecture inception must satisfy.

4. The project brief template (mandatory)

Every delivery project's brief carries:

Field Content
Outcome One sentence: what is true after the project that is not true now
Boundary Out of scope, and which existing surfaces it must not fork
Reuse sources Paths inside conveyor; estate services/modules; upstream crates. Each with the analysis question
Inception units analysis and architecture, the project's first two claims
Dependencies Typed: blocked-by, acceptance-blocked-by, blocks, related
Acceptance BDD scenario per part with a Verify: command
Capabilities The organisation positions/capabilities that own it
Plan The slice/S## plan path, or the T## SPEC_V2 section

5. The inception standard (per delivery project)

Analysis — current state and reuse: for the project's capability, inventory the code that already exists (inside the conveyor repo, in the wider estate, and upstream), the contract and licence constraints, the build-vs-reuse decision, and the risks. It cites path:symbol inside the repo; outside, it names the capability, not a private host or URL.

Architecture — the project's design, produced to the architecture artefact standard (SPEC_V2 §8.1): the aggregates, tables, actions, modules, integrations, decision record and acceptance, plus the artefacts its phase and plateau require — the PSA set at inception (the principles it applies, its requirements, stakeholder map, driver/goal/objective and solution concept) and the HLD and SD sets as it matures (business services, data entities, application and technology views, process and migration diagrams). Every artefact is a typed catalog, matrix or diagram carrying the standard's common metadata — owner, reviewer and review date, version, purpose/stakeholders/scope, the related project, and its published document or contract — and its links to what it derives from, realises or reuses. The project operates under the live architecture_contract that governs it; it must fit the blueprint, and a deviation is a decision record, not a silent fork.

Both are documents on the project with meta.category set. They are published when accepted. A project without both published is an inception gap; its build units are not claimed.

The unit protocol is where that programme context is recorded for every delivery unit: its issue carries the Tranche it is authorised under, the Brief that governs it, the ADM phase/plateau where it changes the architecture, and the Benefits its output is mapped to (docs/plans/03-DELIVERY.md § "Issue content contract"). The end-of-tranche review (§7) reads those four fields from the issue ledger, so the review's evidence is the buildout's own record and not a separate report.

6. Benefit profiles and the benefits map (mandatory)

The programme is judged on benefits operations realizes after the projects close, not on the capabilities it delivers. A capability gives a team a new option; whether it uses the option, and whether use improves performance, are separate questions. A benefit is a measurable improvement resulting from an outcome, valued by at least one stakeholder, that contributes to an organisational objective.

Every benefit profile carries all six groups; a profile missing a baseline, a target, a measurement method or an owner is refused:

Group Fields
Identification and purpose reference, description, category, objectives, observable outcome
Performance affected KPIs, baseline, target, measurement method, financial value where expressible
Cost cost of realizing the benefit, including the associated business change
Conditions capabilities required, outcomes that must already exist, changes to operating practice
Uncertainty and dependencies related risks and issues, dependencies outside the programme
Responsibility and attribution who realizes it, the benefit owner (BCM), the operational area to which the improvement is attributed

The benefits map records each path — project output → capability → operational outcome → benefit or disbenefit → strategic objective — and the dependencies between benefits, on outputs and on capabilities. It answers two questions the programme needs at every review: which benefit is put at risk when a project slips, and which operational area must change its practice before delivery creates value.

The baseline is measured while the old practice is still observable. A benefit whose baseline was never measured cannot claim an improvement, and a project output is not a benefit.

7. Tranches and end-of-tranche reviews

A tranche is the unit the programme manages and reviews together. The PI cadence is the delivery rhythm inside it; the tranche review is a programme decision, not a demo.

Each tranche records its number, entry and exit criteria, the baseline it is managed against and its review date. The end-of-tranche review is an approval: the sponsoring group decides continue, pause or stop on evidence — the baselines, the benefit status against the profiles, the risk and issue register, and the blueprint alignment. A review with no evidence is refused. A re-baseline goes through change control; the review does not rewrite the baseline. The first tranche is authorised only after the definition set is accepted, and no delivery project starts before that authorisation.

The authorisation is the P0 tranche-review output — the last of the P0 class's 21 categories (seed output-standard.json) and the sponsoring group's recorded decision with its evidence. The programme's closure gate (#302) refuses P0's close while that output is unpublished, so the order is enforced by the engine, not remembered by the session.
