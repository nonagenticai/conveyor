The Gemba Walk — client and architecture specification

Status: Proposed · Date: 2026-11-26 · Owner direction: 2026-11-26 · ADR: 0021 Normative siblings: SPEC_V2 §10.5, §10.5.11, §15.1 D64/D66/D67 · Plan: docs/plans/GEMBA-WALK.md

This document specifies a second client of the conveyor — a game-style, spatial interface — as a candidate primary UI. It is written so a future session can build it without reading the local single-file mock, and so a reviewer can tell a real state from a designed one. It is a specification, not an implementation claim: every destination starts absent and is credited only by a route, a spec and the parity instrument.

1. Status and relationship to apps/web

  • The walk is a separate client served at /walk/ from the same image and origin. It adds no port, no table, no action and no event.
  • apps/web remains the normative operator UI until the parity gate in §13 and the owner's primacy decision. A V2 destination is still measured, for D65, from apps/web's route table and its Playwright spec; the walk is measured by its own instrument.
  • "Primary" means the walk owns the default entry point and the operator redirects; it does not mean a second backend, a second decision store or a second set of read models.
  • The design pack at /design/ keeps its role as the public programme site and reviewed reference. The walk is not a pack page and never ships inside /design/.

2. Experience model

The world is navigation; the sheets are the work. The canvas renders rooms, figures and state; every record, table, form, confirmation and decision is a DOM sheet with ordinary HTML semantics. No capability exists only in the world, and no text entry, table or long-form content is rendered inside the canvas or a game engine.

  • Entrance — /walk/ is the arrival surface: the map, the current seat, the queues that need a decision, and a fast path into every wing. It is usable with no pointer and no walk.
  • Wings — the seven navigation groups of SPEC_V2 §10.5.1 (Portfolio & plan; Organisation; Procedures & authoring; Govern & design; Knowledge & memory; Measure & improve; Operations) become wings. A wing is a corridor of rooms; a room is a destination; a desk inside a room is a tab or a sub-screen; a sheet is a record or a task.
  • The first six rooms are the mock's validated set, rebuilt on real data: Architecture, Portfolio, Delivery (the board), Project desk, Coach and Kata. They are evidence that the interaction works, not the limit of the map. Every other destination gets a room before P6.
  • HUD — the principal, the active tenant, connection/stream state, the approval and meeting queues, the command palette, the map, the camera control and help. The HUD is always reachable by keyboard; it is the shortest path to any deep link.
  • Movement — pointer-drag or arrow keys to look, WASD/arrows to move, first/third person toggle with a labelled button and V, wheel zoom, and a boom that shortens near walls rather than clipping. The camera is a preference, never a requirement.
  • Fast travel — the map and the palette move focus to any room or record. A user can complete an entire task through links, the palette and sheets without ever moving the avatar.

3. Seats and authority

  • The principal and memberships come from GET /api/me; the active tenant is sent as X-Active-Tenant on every call.
  • Seats are view presets, not privileges. Delivery (product owner), Programme (project office), Owner (tenant owner) and Reviewer (observer) select a default landing, queue and room emphasis. Changing a seat never changes a permission: the server's manifest, scopes and refusal are the only authority. A control whose action is absent from the manifest is not rendered.
  • The mock's Customer seat is not carried over. Customer care is out of conveyor's scope (D48); the external stakeholder reads the public pack at /design/, not a seat in the walk. The Product owner and Owner presets map to real job keys and memberships; the Programme preset is the project office lens.
  • The walk never infers a position, signs for a user, or pre-selects an approval. It presents the server's gate and the signed-in principal's own choice, exactly as §10.5.4 requires.

4. Information architecture and routing

Route parity by construction. Every apps/web path has a walk path formed by the /walk prefix: /portfolio → /walk/portfolio, /board/:board_id → /walk/board/:board_id, /design/adm → /walk/design/adm. /walk/ is the entrance (the app's /). This makes the parity instrument a set comparison and the old-route redirect map mechanical.

The table is the map, not a substitute for §10.5's contracts: the read/write families, states and refusals are §10.5.2's and the owning slice's. A destination whose slice is not built renders the same not yet live gate as apps/web, naming the missing action or slice, and keeps its route.

Routing rules

  • Clean URLs (/walk/<app path>), HTML5 history. The server must serve /walk/index.html for a non-asset path under /walk/ (see §10); without that change a walk deep link falls through to the operator app's index.html.
  • Query strings are preserved and are part of the deep link. A sheet that represents a record uses a path or query the server can be linked to; a purely visual camera state is never a URL requirement.
  • Back/forward restores the room and the sheet. Closing a sheet returns focus to its opener.
  • A signed-out deep link is a sign-in gate that returns to the same path.

5. Data and action contract

The walk is a contract-faithful client of the existing server; it invents nothing.

  • Manifest. GET /api/actions returns the role-filtered manifest (name, version, kind, spec_hash, scopes, idempotent, timeout_ms). The walk caches it per session and renders a control only when the action is present. A manifest refresh after a 403 is allowed; a control is never shown on a guess.
  • Describe. actions.describe {name} returns input and output JSON Schema plus effect (read | write | destructive), confirm (none | card | card-typed), tenancy, spec_hash and the scope list. This is what makes a generic action sheet possible: the form is generated from the schema, and the effect/confirm/tenancy drive the confirmation and approval UX.
  • Reads. Literal GET routes only (the §10.5.2 read paths), with next_cursor pagination. No GraphQL, no ad-hoc read endpoint, no published snapshot as the app's source.
  • Writes. POST /api/actions/{name}/invocations with {"input": …} and, for a destructive call, {"input": …, "approval_id": "…"}. Headers: Authorization: Bearer, X-Active-Tenant, If-Match: <version> on an update, Idempotency-Key on a create. No optimistic mutation: after a write the screen refetches its read model.
  • Errors. RFC 9457 application/problem+json mapped by status: 401 sign-in, 403 permission, 404 absent, 409 conflict/gate, 412 precondition (refetch; never guess a new version), 422 invalid (field errors shown verbatim), 5xx with request_id. The server's refusal is shown, not replaced by a client rule.
  • Dual control. A gated decision names its approval; the walk never decides on the principal's behalf and never auto-acquires an approval. The approval and meeting queues are read views; the act is the principal's.
  • Live updates. SSE topics (board, card, run, approval) via fetch + ReadableStream because EventSource cannot carry the bearer. A screen without a topic polls and says so. The stream is cancelled on navigation.
  • Cancellation. Every screen load is abortable; a slow response for a left screen is discarded.

6. Client architecture

Dependency rule: destinations may import api, forms, shell and router; shell may import api, router, world/port; world/* imports only world/port and pure helpers. No destination imports another destination. No engine module fetches, decides or stores authority.

6.2 The WorldView port

The world is replaceable. The contract (JSDoc-typed; the exact names are the implementation's):

  • mount(container, options): Promise<void>
  • setScene(scene) — rooms, doors, figures, state badges; derived from read models by the shell.
  • setCamera({mode, focus, zoom}) — first/third, room focus, zoom.
  • setHighlight({roomId, recordId}) — selection from a sheet or the palette.
  • on(event, handler) — enter-room, select, hover, escape; returns an unsubscribe.
  • resize(), dispose().

Constraints for every implementation: no text input, no tables, no long-form content; all scene text is a short label duplicated in the DOM; the canvas is labelled and never traps focus; a pure-DOM map/list view exposes the same rooms and records; no network access; no authority.

6.3 Renderer candidates

  • Baseline — canvas 2D pseudo-3D. The mock's approach: a hand-written projection, painter's depth sort, flat-shaded faces, local textures and procedural figures. It has no engine dependency, no WebGL requirement and a small payload. It is the default until ADR 0022 says otherwise.
  • Candidate — Bevy 0.18, WASM. The world as a Rust/WASM module behind the same port, loaded as a static asset. Use Bevy's 2d/ui feature bundles rather than the default set; keep bevy_ui for labels only (DOM remains the work surface). The build runs on GitHub-hosted CI only (the owner's no-local-Rust rule). If selected, ADR 0022 records the measured reason and the bundle/startup budgets; if not, the canvas baseline stays and no Rust enters the client.

6.4 State

  • Server state — fetched per screen, held in memory, refetched after a write; never written to storage.
  • Session — token in memory; PKCE verifier in sessionStorage; tenant and membership in memory.
  • Per-viewer preferences — last room, camera mode, seat preset, reduced-motion override and help-dismissed, under a conveyor.walk.* key in localStorage. Preferences are conveniences and the app renders correctly when storage is empty or throws.
  • No programme store. The mock's conveyor.gemba.v1 object (walls, cards, issues, decisions, reports) is not carried over. A client-side copy of programme state is a fixture by another name.

6.5 Sheets and forms

  • A sheet is one of: record, list/table, form, confirmation, refusal, history. It has a title, a close control, focus capture and return, and a URL that can be reloaded.
  • The schema form renderer maps JSON Schema to labelled fields (string, number, boolean, enum, array, object, date-time), shows required fields and descriptions, validates before sending where the schema allows, and always lets the server be the authority. An unsupported construct falls back to a labelled JSON editor with parse validation, so no action is unreachable because of a widget.
  • A write shows the effect class: a write confirms once; a destructive action shows the approval requirement and, when the server refuses for a missing approval, the refusal verbatim.
  • A 412 refetches the record and shows both versions' version; it never retries with a guessed If-Match.

8. Accessibility and responsive behaviour

  • Every destination, record, form and decision is reachable by keyboard alone; the skip link precedes the HUD; the palette is a keyboard path to any route and action.
  • The canvas is role="img" with an accessible name and is not focus-trapped; a DOM map/list exposes the same rooms and records. No information exists only in pixels.
  • Sheets manage focus (capture, escape, return), have labelled headings and form fields, and announce refusals with a live region. Tables have captions and header scopes.
  • Contrast meets WCAG 2.2 AA for ink-on-paper and cream-on-wood; focus is always visible.
  • 200% zoom and 390 px width: no horizontal page scroll, 16 px gutters, sheets full-screen, controls at least 44 px.
  • Reduced motion is honoured; autoplay, audio and timed interactions are absent.

9. Performance and security

Performance budgets (provisional; ADR 0022 fixes any engine-specific number from the spike):

  • First load of the shell + entrance: ≤ 250 KB gzip of JS/HTML/CSS, excluding an optional wasm engine; a destination module lazy-loads on first visit.
  • World: 60 fps target on an M-series laptop at the reference scene; no frame over 50 ms after the first two; degrade labels/effects rather than drop input.
  • Sheets: virtualise any table above 200 rows; one read per screen plus its stream, not per row.
  • Memory: a 5-minute soak with a stream and repeated navigation stays within 1.5× the first-load heap; a leaked interval or subscription is a defect.
  • If Bevy is selected, the release wasm and its cold interactive time are measured under the same protocol as the baseline; the engine is a lazy asset, never part of the sign-in path's critical budget.

Security

  • Access token in memory only; PKCE verifier in sessionStorage; never a token in localStorage, a URL or a log. The walk follows apps/web/src/auth.rs's model.
  • Every server string is rendered as text; schema-generated labels and descriptions are escaped. No innerHTML of server data; no eval; no third-party script, font or image origins.
  • The client is same-origin with the API; it sends no request to /api when signed out and holds no embedded credentials.
  • A refusal is displayed with its action code and the server's message; a toast never claims a success the server did not return.
  • The walk adds no CSP weakening. If a strict policy is introduced, it must work under it (no inline script injection, no remote assets).

10. Deployment and operational contract

  • Static sub-site. apps/walk's build output is copied to /out/site/walk in the image. The server already serves a directory with its own index.html as a sub-site (the /design/ model).
  • Deep links. crates/api/src/web.rs currently falls back to the site-root index.html for a non-asset path that is not a directory index, so /walk/portfolio would render the operator app. Add a scoped rule: for GET/HEAD, a path under /walk/ with no file extension serves /walk/index.html. It never applies to /api, /mcp, health or metrics. A Rust test asserts the fallback and the 404 for a missing asset.
  • Local development. apps/walk/dev-server.mjs serves the walk under /walk/ on port 8932 and proxies /api, /health, /config.json and /__dev/login to 127.0.0.1:8930, preserving the production path shape. Playwright uses http://127.0.0.1:8932 as its base URL.
  • Identity. The same issuer and public client; add the walk's redirect URI (https://<host>/walk/, and the dev origin's /walk/) to the client's valid redirect URIs. If the identity operator prefers isolation, register a dedicated public client and serve its id to the walk rather than widening the app's client. This is an external configuration prerequisite, not a repository change.
  • Configuration. /config.json is served at the origin root; the walk fetches it absolutely and uses the same issuer, client_id and dev_auth fields.
  • Caching. The entry document is not cached as a stale build (no-store or the site's existing behaviour); assets may be cached with a content hash or a version query. No new CDN or cache layer.
  • No new port in production. The production entry is the existing origin at /walk/; port 8932 is local development only. Port 8000 is never used.
  • If Bevy is selected. The wasm build (wasm32-unknown-unknown + wasm-bindgen) runs in GitHub-hosted CI, never on a local machine; the output lands in the same static tree. The static contract above does not change.

11. Verification

  • Unit (node:test) — projection, collision, depth sort, scene derivation, route mapping, schema-to-field mapping, problem mapping. Pure modules only; no DOM.
  • Type check — tsc --noEmit over JSDoc-typed modules; a type error fails the walk workflow.
  • Playwright, one spec per destination — empty, error, happy and one refusal (or one write where the destination owns a write), plus manifest gating (a control absent when its action is absent) and the headers on a write (If-Match, Idempotency-Key, approval_id where applicable).
  • Auth — dev login, deep-link gate, sign-out, and a 401 path; the OIDC redirect is exercised in the P0 spike and in a deployed smoke where configured.
  • Parity instrument — scripts/build-walk-coverage.py compares the frozen census, the walk route registry and the walk specs; it emits docs/evidence/walk-coverage.json and exits non-zero on a missing destination, an expired exemption or a reachability gap. The schema and gate are in docs/plans/GEMBA-WALK.md §6.
  • Accessibility — a keyboard-only navigation spec; focus/name assertions; a manual screen-reader pass per wing; contrast checked from computed styles.
  • Performance — the budgets in §9, measured in CI where stable and recorded in the plan's evidence otherwise; the engine protocol in §12 is separate and feeds ADR 0022.

12. The engine decision protocol (P0)

The P0 spike runs the same reference scene (six rooms, 12 figures, labels, one moving figure) on the same machine class, cold cache, five runs, median reported with min–max, under 4× CPU throttling and Slow 4G for load metrics. It records:

Metric Why it matters
release bytes (gzip) and request count first-load budget; wasm is lazy but must be bounded
cold load → first interactive world (ms) whether the walk opens like a page or like a game
steady frame p50/p95 and long tasks whether the world is smooth at the reference scene
heap after 5 minutes and after 20 room changes leaks decide long-session viability
browser requirements (WebGL2/WebGPU, fallback) whether low-end and remote users are excluded
CI build time and reproducibility the no-local-Rust rule makes CI the only build
DOM-overlay input/focus and screen-reader path the engine must not own the work surface

ADR 0022 records the winner and the budgets. The baseline is not deleted when Bevy wins; it remains the fallback behind the port.

13. Parity gate (before any primacy claim)

The walk reaches parity only when all of these are true and recorded:

  1. Every §10.5 destination and every retained V1 route in apps/web/src/app.rs has a walk route and a Playwright spec asserting empty, error, happy and one refusal or write.
  2. Every action in the frozen census is present in the manifest path, describable, and renderable by the schema form (or its JSON fallback); every destination's named write flows round-trip with the correct headers and a refusal path.
  3. The parity instrument reports no missing row and no expired exemption.
  4. The accessibility and performance budgets are met and recorded.
  5. The owner signs the result. Only then may the walk become the default entry point; apps/web is frozen, then retired under the P6 runbook, and the decision is reversible until the deletion.

14. Do-Not list

  • Do not invent a REST endpoint, a read model or an action. Reads are literal GETs; writes are actions.
  • Do not hardcode which actions exist. The manifest is the contract.
  • Do not use the published snapshot, a seed, a fixture or localStorage as the app's programme state.
  • Do not omit Authorization, X-Active-Tenant, If-Match or Idempotency-Key, or send a destructive action without its approval id.
  • Do not render text entry, tables or long-form content in the canvas or the game UI.
  • Do not make walking mandatory for a capability.
  • Do not embed the design pack or copy its 4.7 MB payload; link to /design/.
  • Do not add a table, an action, an event, a decision store or a SPEC_V2 §12 T-row for the walk.
  • Do not infer, pre-select or auto-approve a position; the server's refusal is the UI's answer.
  • Do not put a token in storage or a URL; do not add a third-party origin.
  • Do not claim a destination is implemented from its mock page or its route alone.

15. Non-goals and revisit triggers

Non-goals — a second backend; a second decision store; native mobile apps; chat, audio or video; customer care (D48); replacing /design/; SSR; a public unauthenticated mode in v1; pixel parity with the mock.

Revisit triggers — the parity gate cannot be met without new backend surface; Bevy on the web misses the budgets and the canvas baseline cannot close the gap; two-client maintenance exceeds the value; the owner declines primacy at P6 (the walk stays an alternative client); the operator app's own architecture changes enough that the shared contract or the census must be re-baselined.
