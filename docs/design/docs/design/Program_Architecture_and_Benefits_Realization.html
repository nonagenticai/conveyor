Programme architecture and benefits realization

Status: a public, reconstructed study. The workshop document this programme was designed from was input only and was never committed to this public repository; this page reconstructs its substance from the merged record — docs/SPEC_V2.md §8.1 and §8.3, docs/plans/P0-programme-definition.md §3–§7, and the design pack's programme and architecture layers — and cites what it rests on. The programme state is measured at a stated date; where a fact is a live row, the date is given.

1. Purpose and provenance

The conveyor buildout is delivered as a programme, not as one large project: a temporary organisation that exists to deliver benefits through projects, judged on the improvement operations realizes after those projects close. The design has two interlocking bodies of practice:

  • MSP supplies the programme's definition and its benefit track: the mandate, brief, blueprint, business case, benefits map and profiles, projects dossier, tranches and their reviews, and the measured realization of each benefit (SPEC_V2 §8.3).
  • TOGAF's ADM supplies the architecture capability: the Preliminary phase, the phase rail A–H, the typed artefacts and the contracts that close each phase, compliance and waivers (SPEC_V2 §8.1).

The rigour layer was merged in #216: the V2 rows, the gates, the P0 definition set and the design study. T9 (#81) then built the architecture capability, T10 (#82) the tranche and benefits rows, and T11 (#84) the metrics that measure realization. The design pack's programme and adm screens are the design study; signed in, they read the live rows through the programmes.*, benefits.*, tranches.*, stakeholders.*, cadences.* and architecture.* action families.

Two distinctions run through the whole model and are the reason it is rows rather than a document library:

  1. A capability is not a benefit. A delivered capability gives a team a new option. Whether the team uses it, and whether use improves performance, are separate questions. A benefit is a measurable improvement resulting from an outcome, valued by at least one stakeholder, that contributes to an organisational objective.
  2. A tranche review is a programme decision, not a demo. A demo shows a deliverable; a tranche review decides continue, pause or stop on evidence — the baselines, the benefit status against the profiles, the risk register and the blueprint alignment.

The programme is also a ledger of registrations: a risk, issue, assumption, dependency, benefit or change is a project_register_item of one of six kinds, and a dependency that slips names the benefits it puts at risk through the benefit-map edges. The tranche is the unit the programme manages and reviews together; the PI cadence is the delivery rhythm inside it.

3. Benefits realization

A benefit profile carries six field groups, and a profile missing a baseline, a target, a measurement method or an owner is refused:

  1. Identification and purpose — reference, description, category, objectives, observable outcome.
  2. Performance — the affected KPIs, the baseline, the target, the measurement method and the financial value where it can be expressed.
  3. Cost — the cost of realizing the benefit, including the business change it needs.
  4. Conditions — the capabilities required, the outcomes that must already exist, and the changes to operating practice.
  5. Uncertainty and dependencies — related risks and issues, and dependencies outside the programme.
  6. Responsibility and attribution — who realizes it, the benefit owner (the BCM) and the operational area to which the improvement is attributed.

The benefits map records each path — project output → capability → operational outcome → benefit or disbenefit → strategic objective — and the dependencies between benefits, on outputs and on capabilities. It answers two questions at every review: which benefit is put at risk when a project slips, and which operational area must change its practice before delivery creates value.

The baseline is measured while the old practice is still observable. A benefit whose baseline was never measured cannot claim an improvement, and a project output is not a benefit. The transition record carries the baseline measurement, readiness, enactment, the post-transition measurement and the legacy retirement, with a post-closure owner. A profile's measurement fields, once the profile is approved, change only through a cited, approved change request; a benefit with an open transition cannot be retired.

The programme metrics (T11) are read-time aggregates over those rows: benefits realised versus planned, tranche gate outcomes, the benefit-at-risk count, and the never-refused check. An unmeasured baseline is reported unmeasured, never zero.

4. The TOGAF ADM capability

The ADM is implemented as a capability first and a project second. The Preliminary phase establishes six things before architecture work starts:

  • the architecture principles catalogue — statement, rationale and implications per principle;
  • the tailored ADM profile — which phases run, which artefacts are mandatory per phase and plateau, and which contract closes each phase;
  • the artefact standard and its repository — typed catalogues, matrices and diagrams, each with its common metadata (owner, reviewer, review date, version, purpose, stakeholders, scope, project, and its published document or contract);
  • the Architecture Board — its charter, membership and decision limits;
  • the governance, contract and waiver baseline;
  • the maturity (nine dimensions, 0–5) and skills (seven categories) baselines with their development milestones.

The phase rail A–H then produces typed artefacts: vision and stakeholders at A; the business architecture at B; the information-systems architectures at C; the technology architecture at D; opportunities, solutions and the work packages at E; the migration plan at F; implementation governance at G; and change management at H, which records exactly one change or kata. A phase cannot close while a mandatory artefact is still draft, and a delivery project's design gate is refused when it has no live architecture contract. Reviews record one compliance level; a waiver names its accepting owner and its review date.

The capability is a tailored ADM profile for software delivery, not a claim of TOGAF certification or full conformance; what is claimed is testable: the artefacts and gates the organisation uses are rows, the process runs, and a deviation is a decision record rather than a silent fork.

6. The measurement and refusal discipline

The programme's claims rest on four rules that are enforced in code, not prose:

  • Metrics are computed from rows, never stored as opinion. A change to the source rows changes the figure; reading a metric writes nothing.
  • A gate that never refused is flagged. A gate with recorded decisions and no rejection is reported as never-refused with its counts, so a stamp cannot read as health.
  • A declared output that is unpublished blocks closure. A project cannot close while an output it declared is missing.
  • A retirement is a record, not a delete. A retired benefit or tranche keeps its row and its reason; an open transition blocks the retirement of its benefit.

These are the mechanisms that let a tranche review or a benefit realization be taken on evidence. They are deliberately narrow: rows, gates and read models, with no certification badge and no second register.

7. What this study does not claim

It does not claim MSP or TOGAF certification, or a compliance badge. It does not claim a live metamodel, a portfolio optimiser, a schedule solver or a benefit-forecasting model. It does not replace the charging system: billing_service remains the charging authority, and the conveyor's reconciliation compares its own derived figures with that roll-up and opens an incident on a difference; the transport and its TLS policy belong to the platform's external-action path. And it does not claim that the programme's own benefits are yet realized: at the measured date their baselines are unmeasured, which is exactly what a benefits discipline is supposed to make visible.
