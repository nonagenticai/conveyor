Benefit profiles — P0 (retrospective, 2026-11-26)

Identification and purpose

P0 published six benefit profiles, represented by "Benefit profile — B1 Auditable decisions" (01a1e9e0-1b2e-7353-9361-16a81a99d354, published 2026-11-18 at 08:17:51). Each profile carries a reference, description, category, objectives and observable outcome, so a benefit is identified as a measurable improvement valued by a stakeholder and contributing to an objective, not as a project output.

Performance

The performance group records the affected KPIs, the baseline, the target, the measurement method and the financial value where one is expressible. The baseline rule is explicit: it is measured while the old practice is still observable, and a benefit whose baseline was never measured cannot claim an improvement. On 2026-11-26 the profiles define those fields; the record carries no post-transition performance reading.

Cost

Each profile records the cost of realising the benefit, including the associated business change rather than only the delivering project's cost. The P0 record does not restate a monetary figure for any of the six benefits, so this retrospective reports the field as defined and the amount as unmeasured here. No spend against a benefit is claimed.

Conditions

The conditions group names the capabilities the benefit requires, the outcomes that must already exist, and the changes to operating practice that turn use into improvement. The public/opt-in licensing frame is one such condition, and each benefit names the operational area that must change. The capability is the option; the conditions are what the programme must still cause to happen.

Uncertainty and dependencies

Each profile records related risks and issues and the dependencies outside the programme, and the benefits map supplies the edges that identify which benefit is at risk when a dependency slips. P0 records the uncertainty structure but does not score it. The 2026-11-26 position is defined dependencies and no quantified risk exposure.

Responsibility and attribution

Every profile names who realises the benefit, the benefit owner — the BCM — and the operational area to which the improvement is attributed. That attribution is what separates a realised benefit from a claimed one. What is measurable now is the assignment recorded in the six published profiles; realisation is not claimed for any of them.
