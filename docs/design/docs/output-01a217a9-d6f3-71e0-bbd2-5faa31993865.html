Benefits Diagram — SPEC-V2 (retrospective, 2026-11-26)

Goals and outcomes

The goal is an organisation the conveyor owns and can prove: positions that are catalogued, released and bound; work that moves from programme to run with a baseline, a register and evidence; and decisions that pass rule → laya → generation → human with every step recorded. The measurable outcome is auditability at the level of a single decision — which option, which confidence, which policy, which person, which evidence (§13). The plan calls this the unglamorous win, and it is the outcome the benefit depends on.

Measures

The measures are computed read models, never stored opinions (D37): flow (lead time, cycle time, queue time, block time, flow efficiency, WIP, throughput), quality (first-pass yield, rework rate, defect escapes, eval score, benchmark delta), cost from the ledger, governance (gate latency, approval rate, override rate, evidence completeness, never-refused gates) and knowledge (pack hit rate, citation rate, readiness blocks, lesson promotion). Metering rolls up at tenant, department, programme, project and job and drills to run, step and call, reconciled with billing_service (D52). A gate that never refused in a full cycle is flagged as broken.

Benefits and sub-benefits

The benefit is auditability at the level of a single decision. Its sub-benefits are a staffed improvement loop — coach, kata, development, measurement and adoption as one loop (§9.4); a programme cadence in which a PI is the delivery rhythm and a tranche is the managed unit, with the review an approval carrying baselines, benefit status and the risk register (§8.2, §8.3); and executable rigour, with TOGAF, SAFe, PMP and MSP as rows and gates and no certification claim (§8.6). A benefit without baseline, target, method and owner is refused, so no sub-benefit can be claimed on a wish.
