Review cycle

The benefits are reviewed at every tranche review (P0 plan §7), against the baseline and the profiles, together with the risk and issue register and the blueprint alignment. A review with no evidence is refused. The benefits at risk are read from the benefits map: a slipping project names the benefits it puts at risk. A re-baseline goes through change control, never through the review.

Roles and assessment

  • The BCM owns each benefit and its transition. The position is unstaffed; the programme manager is the interim owner, and the attribution moves when the position is staffed (SA-1's S1).
  • The SRO (sponsor) is accountable for the coherent capability and the benefits; the sponsor accepts the definition set and decides at each tranche review.
  • The reviewers assess the evidence before a publication; an overstated benefit is returned.

Realization process

For each benefit the BCM: measures the baseline while the old practice is still observable; monitors against the profile; plans the transition; communicates the change; assesses readiness; enacts it; measures the post-transition performance; retires the superseded practice and system; and keeps measuring until the realization plan names who continues after programme closure. The comparison with the baseline is the evidence the sponsor judges. A benefit whose baseline was never measured cannot claim an improvement — the rule the six katas serve.

Gap. The BCM is unstaffed, so the interim programme manager holds the process; no baseline has been measured; the review cycle's first run has not happened. The strategy names the process and the gaps rather than asserting a cycle that has not run.
